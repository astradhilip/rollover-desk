## What I looked for and didn't find

- **Hours analysts spend cleaning rent rolls.** No measured figure. That is why time savings appear only as assumption 1 in [ASSUMPTIONS.md](ASSUMPTIONS.md), with a way to measure it.
- **The firm's internal tools.** Nothing public. The build makes no claim about what already exists. If a good parser is already licensed, its output can enter through the `ISpreadsheetReader` port.
- **Real rent rolls.** None used. The synthetic formats are modelled on public descriptions of property-management exports, broker stacking plans and accounting extracts. Assumption 8 covers the risk that real files are messier.

## How the research shaped the build

- **Loan maturity:** the loan-maturity numbers led to a property-level loan date. That date drives the lease-before-loan flag and a risk component.
- **Notice dates:** the notice-window evidence made the notice date, not the expiry, the first critical date. A lease without a notice term shows it as missing rather than assuming one.
- **Existing parsers:** they made parsing alone not worth building. The value is in visible doubt, explainable numbers and a live workbook. See [PROBLEM.md](PROBLEM.md).
- **Reconciliation:** the rent roll vs lease evidence put lease reconciliation at the top of the "next" list, and out of scope here.
